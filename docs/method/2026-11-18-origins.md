# Independent origins: do Watchfloor's flags rest on one syndicated story? (18 Nov 2026)

*Measured by a Claude session on brief B of `_ideas\drafts\2026-11-17-items-4-6-session-prompts.md`, 18 Nov 2026.
Free: GDELT downloads, a read-only copy of the bucket's `countries.json` and `summary.json` (and the zone-alarm days'
`stories.jsonl`), no bucket writes, no model calls. Code: `pipeline/origins.py`, `scripts/origins_backtest.py`,
`tests/test_origins.py`. Nothing is in the live pipeline.*

## Bottom line

1. **Syndication barely touches the country measures.** Over 17 Oct to 17 Nov, a country-day's articles were 95%
   independent origins (median over days; flagged country-days 93%). GDELT Events keeps one row per event with the
   first article that reported it, so copies of a wire story mostly never reach the `urls` and `violent` counts.
2. **Few flags rest on one or two origins, and those sat on the line.** Collapsing a flagged day's largest two
   clusters to one article each removed 8 of 153 Rising flags on attention (5%) and 10 of 73 on violence (14%).
   Every removed flag had z between 2.50 and 2.71, and fell by 0.3 or less, except one: Australia's violence on
   21 Oct (z 2.67 to 1.97), where one Australian Community Media column ran on 30 sites and GDELT coded it violent.
   The same network moved Australia on 20 Oct (z 3.59 to 2.62, still flagged).
3. **Scored on origins against origins** (same estimator, same Rising rule, same article minimum), 133 of 154
   attention flags and 62 of 74 violence flags stay; 21 and 12 drop, 0 and 6 are new. That is the size of the
   change a switch would make: about one flag in seven, mostly at the threshold.
4. **Bahrain is real reporting, not syndication.** 15 Nov violence: 85 articles, 78 origins, largest cluster 5
   ("Iran claims drone attack on US radar base in Bahrain"); live z 3.52 (21x), origins z 3.78. 13 Nov violence:
   26 articles, 25 origins; live z 2.37 (6.5x, under Rising since the 16 Nov calibration), origins z 2.85. The
   case note's 5.3x / z 3.1 and 31.6x / z 5.7 are the pre-calibration figures.
5. **Zones:** **the same, and already done.** Zone attention counts headline-filtered stories, which `stories.py` already groups (title Jaccard >= 0.6). Regrouped at 80% overlap, the 18 zone alarms of 17 Oct to 17 Nov keep 90-100% of their stories as separate origins (largest cluster 3 stories). Collapsing the top two clusters removes 3 alarms, all on the 2.0 line: Somalia 31 Oct (z 2.00 to 1.92), Sanctions and Shadow Fleet 17 Oct (2.01 to 1.99), Iran & Red Sea 3 Nov (2.00 to 1.99). Rows: `data/origins/zones.json`.

**Recommendation.** Do not replace article counts with origins in Watchfloor's z-scores: it would move about one
flag in seven, almost all at the threshold, for a cost in code and a second baseline. Carry the origin count
beside the article count on country items (`origins`, cheap at build time if the build keeps URLs) so a reader can
see when a flag rests on one story, and fix the one real failure differently: the Australian and UK local
networks (ACM, Newsquest) are a domain table, not a clustering problem. Tom's call.

## Method

- **Data.** Raw GDELT Events 2.0 files, both streams, 19 Sep to 17 Nov 2026 (60 days, all 96 + 96 files each),
  filtered as `events.record` does (RB folded into RI, events dated more than two days before the file skipped).
  Per country-day: distinct article URLs, and the same for violent events (CAMEO 18-20). Our per-day distinct count
  matches the stored count within one or two (the store sums distinct per file).
- **Origins** (`pipeline/origins.py`). Text is the URL slug (Events rows carry no title), normalised as in
  `stories.py`. One origin = one cluster of texts with word overlap >= 80% (`|A & B| / min`), OSINT Swarm's rule,
  with group-average linkage (Europe Media Monitor): a text joins only if its mean overlap with the cluster's members
  is >= 80%. Texts under four words join only an identical text; URLs with no usable slug (13% of articles, mostly
  numeric-id sites) are each their own origin, so origins err high and the "rests on one or two" counts are lower
  bounds. Not done: cross-language linking and EMM's linking of related clusters within 7 days.
- **Test A** (the stored flags). For each stored Rising flag, 17 Oct to 17 Nov (lists as stored today, so
  re-scored days use the calibrated z), the day's count with the largest cluster collapsed to one article, then the
  largest two, scored with `countries.score` against the stored 28-day history and pooled profile. A flag "rests on
  one (or two) origins" when the collapsed count no longer meets the Rising rule.
- **Test B** (origins against origins). From the raw files alone: every catalogued country-day scored twice with
  robust-1, once on articles and once on origins, each against its own 28-day history and pooled profile; Rising
  rule unchanged, article minimum (40 / 15) kept on articles.
